Week 3 · In-Class Exercise

Build a Product Landing Page

Your Challenge

Use the client brief to sketch a product landing page that is clear, persuasive, and focused on one next action.

Time15–20 minutes
FormatPaper or a digital design tool
PriorityClear strategy before polish
Client Brief

SproutSide

SproutSide is a Nashville startup that makes indoor gardening easy. Its Kitchen Garden Mini helps people grow fresh herbs without a yard or gardening experience.

Brand Foundation

  • Purpose: Make home-grown ingredients practical in small spaces.
  • Personality: Calm, practical, and encouraging.
  • Positioning: The simple indoor garden for busy beginners.
  • Primary goal: Motivate qualified visitors to preorder the product.
Buyer Persona

Meet Elena

Elena Ruiz, 29, rents an apartment and enjoys cooking. She wants fresh herbs but has little space, no yard, and often forgets to water plants.

What Matters to Her

  • Fresh ingredients without a complicated routine
  • A clean product that fits her apartment
  • Confidence that a beginner can succeed

Research and Objections

She searches for “small indoor herb garden” and watches short product demos. She worries about mess, maintenance, space, and price.

Product Details and Selling Points

Kitchen Garden Mini costs $89 and includes three herb starter pods. Preorders include free shipping and replacement pods if the first seedlings do not sprout.

Fits Small SpacesOnly 12 inches wide.
Beginner FriendlySelf-watering with a built-in grow light.
Fresh Herbs, FasterReady to harvest in about 30 days.

Create the Mockup

  1. Choose the message. Write a short headline and supporting sentence for Elena.
  2. Choose the action. Make one call to action stand out. You may use Preorder Your Garden.
  3. Sketch the page. Use a familiar structure, clear chunks, and whitespace. Boxes may stand in for images.

Checklist for Success

SproutSide and the Kitchen Garden Mini
A short, benefit-focused headline
A supporting sentence
A compelling product-image placeholder
One visually distinctive primary call to action
Chunked selling points
One trust element or answer to an objection

Connect Week 2 Foundations to Week 3 Design

  • Brand: Make the page feel calm, practical, and beginner-friendly.
  • Customer: Address Elena’s needs and objections.
  • Goal: Keep the page focused on preorders.
  • UX: Use hierarchy, contrast, whitespace, chunking, and a clear call to action.

Be Ready to Explain

  1. Which detail about Elena most influenced your page?
  2. What did you make most visually distinctive?
