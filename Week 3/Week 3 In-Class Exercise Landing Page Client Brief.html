Week 3 · In-Class Exercise

Build a Product Landing Page

Your Challenge

Use the client brief below to create a low-fidelity mockup of a product landing page. Make the page easy to understand, persuasive for the buyer persona, and focused on one clear next action.

DeliverableOne landing-page mockup
FormatPaper or a digital design tool
PriorityClear strategy before polish
Client Brief

SproutSide

SproutSide is a small Nashville startup that makes indoor gardening approachable for people with limited space and limited time. Its first product, the Kitchen Garden Mini, is a compact countertop garden designed to help customers grow fresh herbs without a yard or gardening experience.

Brand Foundation

  • Purpose: Make fresh, home-grown ingredients practical in small spaces.
  • Personality: Optimistic, calm, practical, and encouraging.
  • Positioning: The simple indoor garden for busy beginners.
  • Primary goal: Motivate qualified visitors to preorder the product.
Buyer Persona

Meet Elena

Elena Ruiz, 29, rents a city apartment and enjoys cooking after work. She likes the idea of fresh herbs but has little counter space, no outdoor area, and a history of forgetting to water plants.

What Matters to Her

  • Fresh ingredients without a complicated routine
  • A clean, attractive product that fits her apartment
  • Proof that a beginner can keep the herbs alive

Research and Objections

She searches for “small indoor herb garden” and watches short product demos on Instagram and YouTube. She worries about mess, maintenance, counter space, and whether the product is worth the price. She usually researches on her phone and completes larger purchases on her laptop.

Product Details and Selling Points

Kitchen Garden Mini launches at $89 and includes basil, mint, and cilantro starter pods. Every preorder includes free shipping and replacement pods if the first seedlings do not sprout.

Fits Small SpacesA 12-inch footprint makes it practical for apartment counters.
Beginner FriendlyA self-watering reservoir and built-in grow light remove the guesswork.
Fresh Herbs, FasterCustomers can begin harvesting common herbs in about 30 days.

Create the Mockup

  1. Choose the message. Write one short headline and one supporting sentence that connect the product’s value to Elena’s needs.
  2. Plan the hierarchy. Decide what Elena should notice first, second, and third. Select one clear call to action; suggested wording is Preorder Your Garden.
  3. Sketch the landing page. Organize the information using a familiar page structure, clear chunks, and enough whitespace. Use boxes or labels as image placeholders.
  4. Check the mobile experience. Add one note explaining how your design would adapt at approximately 600 pixels wide.

Your Mockup Must Include

SproutSide and the Kitchen Garden Mini
A short, benefit-focused headline
A supporting sentence
A compelling product-image placeholder
One visually distinctive primary call to action
Three chunked selling points
One trust element or answer to an objection
A note about the 600-pixel mobile layout

Connect Week 2 Foundations to Week 3 Design

  • Brand and positioning: Make the visual and verbal choices feel optimistic, calm, and beginner-friendly.
  • Customer: Prioritize Elena’s needs, likely questions, search behavior, and purchase objections.
  • Goal: Keep the page focused on preorder conversion rather than giving every possible action equal emphasis.
  • UX and layout: Answer who the company is, why the product is valuable, and what the visitor should do next. Apply hierarchy, contrast, alignment, proximity, whitespace, chunking, familiar patterns, and a distinctive call to action.

Be Ready to Explain

  1. Which buyer-persona insight most influenced your page?
  2. What did you make most visually distinctive, and why?
  3. How did you reduce cognitive load and keep the action clear?
