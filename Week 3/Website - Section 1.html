Takeaways

  • We have to accept human behavior for what it is, not what we want it to be. Human-Centered-Design (HCD) puts human needs, capabilities, and behaviors first, and then designs in a way to accommodate those needs, capabilities, and behaviors.
  • Humans can only process so much information at a time. The human brain takes shortcuts. Much of our design is based on these concepts.

Human-Centered Design (HCD)

Source Excerpt from: https://uxplanet.org/gestalt-principles-in-ux-design-2e0f423bfcb5

Instead of forcing users to adapt to product design, we design the technology in a way that is adapted to people.

  • "Why are they doing that??"
  • We have to accept human behavior for what it is, not what we want it to be.
  • We are designing technology things for people, so we need to understand both technology and people.

Human-Centered Design puts the human needs, capabilities, and behaviors first, and then designs in ways to accommodate those needs, capabilities, and behaviors.

  • HCD starts with an understanding of humans and human behavior. This partly has to be learned from observation, because humans aren't always aware of their reasoning or motivation behind doing things. They may not even be aware that they're operating in a certain way.

Short-Term Memory

Source Excerpt from: Book The Design of Everyday Things

Short-term or working memory (STM) retains the most recent experiences or material that is currently being thought about. It is the memory of the "just present". Information is retained automatically and retrieved without effort, but the amount of information that can be retained this way is severely limited.

The limit of STM is approximately 5 to 7 items, with the number going to 10 to 12 if the numbers are constantly being repeated. (Or you're using special techniques like mnemonics to remember it.)

Miller's Law

Miller's Law states that the average person can keep only 7 (+/-2) items in their working memory.

Cognitive Load: The amount of mental resources needed to understand and interact with an interface.

  • This capacity can change at different times.
