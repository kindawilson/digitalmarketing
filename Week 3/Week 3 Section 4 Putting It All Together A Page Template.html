Section 4

Putting It All Together: A Page Template

Combine the Laws of UX and design principles into a clear landing-page framework.

Key Takeaways

  • A strong landing page quickly explains who you are, why the offer matters, and what to do next.
  • Use a compelling visual, brief headline, short support, clear call to action, and generous white space.
  • Treat the template as a proven starting point, then change it intentionally.

These examples appeared on lists of well-designed websites, and they follow a recognizable template. That consistency is not accidental: familiar structure, strong hierarchy, limited choices, and generous space help people act.

A template is a starting point, not a rule. Use it to establish clarity, then deviate only when the customer and the goal justify the change.

1. Decide the Key Information

A landing page or homepage needs to answer three essential questions:

  1. Who or what are you?
  2. Why should customers buy from you?
  3. What should they do next?

2. Present It Clearly

For the design above the fold Above the fold refers to information visible when a visitor first lands on a page, before scrolling. The phrase comes from the content visible on the front page of a folded newspaper., remember KISS: Keep It Simple.

  • Include one vivid, compelling image.
  • Put the most important idea in a short headline.
  • Add a small amount of supporting information.
  • If more detail is necessary, chunk it.
  • Provide one clear call to action.
  • Leave plenty of white space.
