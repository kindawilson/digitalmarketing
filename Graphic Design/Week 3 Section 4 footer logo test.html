Section 4

Putting It All Together: A Page Template

Key Takeaways

  • A landing page or homepage should quickly answer who you are, why customers should buy, and what to do next.
  • Use a compelling image, short headline, supporting information, clear call to action, and whitespace.

Look at these examples below from website landing pages. Click through the images in the carousel and compare them. What do you see in common?

If you think they look similar, you’re right. All of these examples actually ended up on a list of best-designed websites. And they do seem to be following a template of sorts.

Think of all of the Laws of UX and design principles that we’ve covered so far. If you put them all together, you end up with many similar-feeling landing pages and homepages. And if you’re beginning in the digital marketing world, you can use these templates to help guide your designs.

That’s not to say that you can’t stray from this layout for a homepage. But there’s a reason so many companies use a similar format: because it’s effective. Use this template as a way to get started. And then strategically pivot from it when needed.

Let’s step through the process below of building a homepage.

1. Decide the key information to include

Remember that we want to focus on the most important items. That’s it. A landing page or homepage really only needs to answer three questions. Think through these questions below.

Answer the questions:

  1. Who / what are you?
  2. Why should customers buy from you?
  3. What to do next

2. Use the design principles to clearly present it

For the design “above the fold” Above the fold refers to information that is showing on the screen when you land on a page. Much like the information that would show on the front page of a news paper when you “fold” it. (Remember KISS - Keep It Simple, Stupid):

  • Include a vivid, compelling image
  • Put the most important thing in a short headline
  • Put a short bit(s) of supporting information
  • If you have more details you need to include, chunk it
  • Provide a clear call to action
  • (and leave plenty of whitespace)
  • …and that’s it!
