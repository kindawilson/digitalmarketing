Section 5

Creating a Search Campaign

Key Takeaways

  • A campaign workflow connects the goal, settings, ad group, keyword choices, ad content, budget, and measurement.
  • Manual keyword bidding requires a deliberate maximum CPC for each selected keyword and careful budget monitoring.

In the previous example, you set a daily budget to run the ad. This is common, and our expert discussion video this week even mentions doing that. In that scenario, Google uses an algorithm to decide how much to bid on each keyword.

However, there is an option to manually enter the maximum price you want to spend for bidding on each keyword. Although this takes much more time than automated bidding, there are certain situations where it might be strategic. There might be a maximum amount you want to spend on a certain keyword. Keep in mind that some keywords might cost in the tens or hundreds of dollars each! That might be a scenario where you want to make sure you’re paying per click and not just a daily budget.

The manual keyword bidding will also be the scenario for the SIMternship that you’ll be going through.

Let’s walk through that process. In the scenario below, you can see where you search for keywords and add them (as you would do in a non-manual-keyword bidding situation). The difference here is, you will see the average cost per click (CPC) for each keyword. A good strategy (here and in the SIM) would be to set your maximum CPC bid for each keyword at a little above the average CPC.

It’s not a good idea to set the maximum CPC really, really high. Competitors can scoop your bid data and cause you to blow through your budget.

For the keyword lists, again you’re ideally looking for highly relevant keywords that have high search volume and low competition. And in search ads, we add that we ideally want a low CPC.
