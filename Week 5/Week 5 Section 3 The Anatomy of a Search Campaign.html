Section 3

The Anatomy of a Search Campaign

Key Takeaways

  • A search campaign contains one or more ad groups organized around closely related search themes.
  • Each ad group aligns a focused keyword list with at least one relevant ad and landing-page intent.

Open this lesson in full screen

As mentioned previously, the search ad is widely applicable and the most widely used. We’re going to take a focused look at creating a search ad campaign over the next couple of sections. You want to make sure that you have not only a handle on it, but some experience in trying it out.

Let’s start with the basics of what a search ad looks like and the different pieces of it. This will be important because we will be strategically building all of these pieces soon.

In a search campaign, we “bid” on certain keywords against other competitors. When our customer searches for that keyword, then our search ad appears, usually above the organic search results. It’s common for a marketer to be charged each time the customer clicks on the ad, known as “Pay Per Click”.

You’ll also want to know a few key terms in a search ad campaign. First of all, you will have a campaign goal such as sales, leads, or website traffic (some of those metrics we mentioned earlier).

The ad campaign itself contains one or more ad groups. An ad group organizes one closely related search theme. It keeps the keywords, ad message, and landing-page intent aligned. Each ad group contains a keyword list and at least one related ad. This is the part that can sound a little confusing at first, but it starts to make sense as you see the structure and understand its purpose. The point of marketing is to know our buyer persona(s) and to structure our keyword searches and ads around their needs. It makes sense that we should be able to strategically group keywords with their related ads and landing pages.
