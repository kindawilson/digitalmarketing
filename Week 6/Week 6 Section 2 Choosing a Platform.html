Section 2

Choosing a Platform

Key Takeaways

  • Choose platforms based on your customers and the content that supports your goals.
  • Use competitor research as insight, not a constraint.

Open this lesson in full screen

Now that we’ve talked about our overall goals and metrics for social media, let’s look at how to choose a platform. This will likely be at least somewhat intuitive for you. When you choose a platform, you need to look at where your customers are and which platform(s) fit the purpose or use that you need (we can also look at it as the type of content that aligns with your goals).

When choosing platform(s), ask yourself:

  1. Where are your customers or potential customers?
  2. Which platform fits the type of content that aligns with your goals / the purpose or use that you need?

When stepping through this with a company or product, you might want to print out our company social media worksheet to brainstorm ideas.

2. Looking at Platform Content and Use

It’s also important to look at what type of content is being shared and what each platform is used for. For instance, LinkedIn is seen as a professional networking platform, so B2B content such as industry articles would work really well there. Instagram and TikTok are highly visual platforms, with both having substantial video usage. These could work really well for content that has an artistic flair and is expressed well visually (example: cake decorators, painters, etc.).

X (Twitter) is great for bite-sized information and real-time reporting….and Q&A. So a company that wants to regularly update its followers and also field customer complaints might consider including it as a platform to use (example: an airline).

These aren’t the limitations of these platforms, but they do give us a good guide for what works well on each.

Companies are not using social media “on the side” anymore – these channels are tightly aligned with concrete business goals.

What goals sit behind this platform mix

Overall, this platform priority supports main corporate objectives:​

  1. Brand awareness
    • Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok are best for reach and mental availability.
    • Visual and video formats (Reels, Shorts, Stories, thumbnails) ensure the brand is repeatedly seen and remembered.
  2. Recruitment and employer branding
    • LinkedIn is the primary driver here, with Facebook and Instagram as supporting stages for behind‑the‑scenes and team stories.​
    • Companies showcase culture, benefits and career paths to position themselves as attractive employers.
  3. Customer insights and feedback
    • Comments, DMs, reviews, polls and Q&As provide continuous input on products, services and campaigns.
    • X/Twitter, Facebook groups, Instagram Stories (polls, question stickers) and community platforms help brands listen and react quickly.​
  4. (Added) Positioning / Credibility and expertise
    • YouTube and LinkedIn provide longform options to establish credibility - YouTube in a video format and LinkedIn with longer articles and posts.
    • Instagram and Facebook allow for shorter, more informal ways to showcase brand expertise or quality.

3. Looking at Competitors on Social Media

A side note here that we can also look at our competitors to see where they are on social media and the type of content they’re sharing. You may get good ideas as to the type of content that is successful and the type of content customers respond to.

You might also get a good idea for which platforms your competitors are getting the engagement with followers on.

However, I’d like to add a word of caution to not limit yourself to the platforms your competitors are on. Some companies haven’t fully optimized their potential on social media. They might be overlooking great opportunities. So research where your competitors are and use this as information to see overall situation. Consider it insight - not a hard-line constraint.
