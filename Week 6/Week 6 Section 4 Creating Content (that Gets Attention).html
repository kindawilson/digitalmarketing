Section 4

Creating Content (that Gets Attention)

Key Takeaways

  • Social media content should support company goals and provide value to the audience.
  • Research formats and use performance feedback to refine content.

Open this lesson in full screen

Now let’s look at actually creating content. When we create content, there will be some trial and error in posting and adjusting. That’s why it’s important to track our KPIs to see which content is getting engagement with our audience and adapt accordingly.

What we do not want to do is to make every post a call-to-action sales post. This will get old fast to your customers. People are on social media to be….social. Use this section to be creative and look for other types of content that will support your company, and only use CTA posts 10-20% of the time at most.

As a review, we want to look at those goals for our social media content as we decide on what would be ideal to post. For example, if we want to brand/position our line of handmade leather cowboy boots and establish its quality and craftsmanship, we might consider posts showing the handmade process behind the scenes.

In this section, we’re going to not only look at what type of content we might consider, but also ways to increase engagement.

Here are the factors we will be considering:

  1. What goals did we have for social media content?
  2. What aligns with our brand?
    1. What showcases our strengths?
  3. What appeals to our customer?
  4. What is a format that will increase engagement?

As a recap, here are a few of those goals that a company might have on social media, with a few notes about each. Keep in mind that these goals can overlap, with content hitting more than one goal:

  • Increase brand awareness - any content that gains traction can organically increase brand awareness. This includes humorous posts, inspirational, etc.
  • Increase top-of-mind awareness - simply being seen regularly on social media can increase top-of-mind awareness. Therefore, if you post regularly, and it gets engagement enough to be seen organically, you’re helping to establish top-of-mind awareness
  • Build community - platforms and content that encourages communication between customers helps to build community
  • Build customer relationship - content that answers questions, helps gain customer insight, or increases engagement between the company and customer helps to build a relationship
  • Brand / rebrand (see also: building credibility / expertise) - content that showcases salient product attributes, product quality, the process of product research/creation, showcasing employees, or even showing customer feedback all tell the customer something about your product/service quality.
  • Improve customer service - answering customer complaints on social media in a very timely matter helps strengthen customer service.
  • Gain customer insight - customer feedback on social media (and social listening) helps give us insight into what customers value. We can use posts to run polls and get feedback for how to adjust products or even which products to offer in the future! (Some clothing companies are now doing polls with customers before purchasing the next season’s styles - to see which appeals to buyers. I’ve seen them have live social media videos from buyers’ markets to ask about specific clothing items!)
  • Establish expertise / credibility (see note above on branding)
  • Increase sales - we can also do a direct sales push on social media, although this should be done sparingly. Aim for 80%+ of your content to be other content that’s not a direct call to action - even showcasing products.

As we go through the following exercises, we’re going to be looking specifically at video posts (or possibly images). They’re the type of posts that are able to most easily add an emotional element and showcase a product.

Before we start strategizing, brainstorm these questions:

  1. What unique company/product characteristics do we have that might be interesting on social media?
    • This could include: product characteristics, mascot personality, unique product creation process, etc.
  2. What human element do we have that might be interesting, entertaining, educational, inspirational, etc
    • This could include: Customer stories, employees, showcasing customer use, etc.

Keep that in mind as we do the following exercise.

I’m now going to challenge you to find a way to create the most engaging content in your industry. We’re now going to brainstorm which frameworks (overall type of video/content structure) we can use to ramp up our content engagement.

So many times we assume that certain types of companies or products are “boring” and can’t gain follower engagement. In the book, The Guide to Going Viral, author Brendan Kane posits instead that frameworks can be used in any industry. The book’s central recommendation is to find a repeatable storytelling format, study why some examples outperform others, and test it with your own audience. Brendan Kane calls these structures Viral Formats. A format is the way a story is presented—not merely its topic.

He lays out the science behind using frameworks for massive customer engagement, regardless of the industry. (Free PDF download of the book at: https://brendanjkane.com/the-guide-to-going-viral/ )

Our goal will be to grab attention, hold attention, and then monetize the attention.

Step through the process below of finding a framework that gain traction in your industry. As with everything, make sure that the framework will reinforce your branding and appeal to your customers. You might even see if one of the frameworks fits really well with a USP that you’ve developed.

Once you have a framework or two that you think will work, try it out! Test out a post or two to see if you get audience engagement.
