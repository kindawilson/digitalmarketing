SEO Terms

As we mentioned, SEO is the process of structuring your site, creating content, and using other strategies to get your site to rank higher in search engines. Let’s make a couple of distinctions here before we move on.

You may also hear the term Search engine marketing (SEM). Search engine marketing (SEM) is a digital marketing strategy used to increase the visibility of a website in search engine results pages (SERPs).

While the industry term once referred to both organic search activities such as search engine optimization (SEO) and paid, it now refers almost exclusively to paid search advertising.

Search engine marketing is also alternately referred to as paid search or pay per click (PPC).

So, what’s the difference between SEO and SEM? Technically, SEM encompasses SEO.

  • SEO: Focuses on driving organic traffic from search engines.
  • SEM: Involves driving both organic and paid traffic from search engines.

Things can get a bit confusing because many people today use SEM interchangeably with PPC. This can make it seem like SEO is being left out, but that’s not the case. SEO is just as much a part of marketing as PPC is.

SEO VS PPC

PPC, or Pay-Per-Click, is a type of digital marketing where advertisers bid on specific keywords they want their ads to show up for in search results. When someone searches for those keywords, the ads appear at the top of the results page and if clicked on the advertisers pay-per-click.

It's crucial not to frame it as "SEO vs. PPC" in terms of which one is better. They complement each other and work best when used together, budget permitting.

To succeed in SEO, you first need to understand how search engines operate. Google, Bing, and other search engines use complex algorithms to find, organize, and rank content—determining what users see when they search.

How Search Engines Work

Search engines follow a three-step process to figure out which items they should deliver to users:

  1. Crawling: Search engines scan the internet using bots (called “spiders” or “crawlers”) to discover new and updated content.
  2. Indexing: Once crawled, pages are stored in a massive database (index), where search engines analyze their content, keywords, and structure.
  3. Ranking: When a user searches, the search engine retrieves the most relevant pages based on hundreds of ranking factors, such as content quality, backlinks, and user experience. (When people search for terms, the search engine returns a Search Engine Results Page, often called SERP.)

Understanding this process helps marketers optimize their websites to ensure search engines can find, index, and rank their pages effectively.
