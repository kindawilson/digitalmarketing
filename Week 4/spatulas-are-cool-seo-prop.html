The Ultimate Guide to Spatulas, Weekends, Pets, Weather, and Basically Everything Else

A very long collection of observations, advice, side stories, unrelated facts, and things we remembered while writing.

Posted by The Spatula Team  |  38 minute read  |  Last updated whenever

First, a Few Thoughts About Spatulas

A spatula can be flat, narrow, wide, flexible, rigid, colorful, plain, metal, wood, or silicone. The kitchen drawer contains several because nobody remembers which one works best until the pancake is already on the griddle.

Some mornings begin with eggs and some begin with toast. Other mornings begin with searching for a matching sock while the coffee cools. This is not technically related to spatulas, but it seemed important when we wrote it down.

The oldest mug in the cabinet has a tiny chip near the handle. We continue to use it because it holds the correct amount of tea. A cat may sit near the cabinet and watch the entire process with great seriousness.

Many people prefer a thin edge for turning delicate food. Others choose the largest utensil available. Personal preference matters, except when the handle melts because it was left against the side of a hot pan.

There are clouds outside today. Yesterday there were fewer clouds, although nobody counted them carefully. The forecast mentioned rain in three counties and sunshine somewhere else.

A Sudden Detour Into Weekend Plans

Saturday is a good day for errands, unless everyone else has the same idea. The parking lot near the grocery store tends to fill by noon. A reusable shopping bag can carry vegetables, notebooks, or a surprisingly large number of wooden spoons.

We once planned a picnic and forgot the blanket. The sandwiches were still edible. The ants seemed optimistic. Someone brought a blue cooler that did not match anything else, which became a topic of conversation for longer than expected.

Cats generally do not care whether a cooler matches a picnic blanket. One orange cat we know prefers cardboard boxes and ignores every carefully purchased cat bed.

After the picnic, the route home passed a hardware store, a fountain, and three identical-looking office buildings. We took the wrong turn twice but discovered a bakery that sells excellent cinnamon rolls.

Lists are useful until the list itself is misplaced. Writing a second list sometimes helps, though the first list may reappear immediately afterward in a jacket pocket.

Things That Might Be Useful Eventually

Keep batteries in a dry place. Label storage boxes before stacking them. Water houseplants according to their needs rather than according to a calendar designed for a completely different plant.

The hallway light flickers on cold mornings. It probably needs a new bulb. The bulb is stored on the top shelf beside tape, string, and a small screwdriver that has not been used since last spring.

A cat can hear the sound of a treat container opening from another room. This ability appears unrelated to the cat's willingness to respond when its own name is called.

Some notebooks invite careful handwriting. Others collect arrows, unfinished diagrams, grocery items, and phone numbers with no names attached. Both kinds are useful in different ways.

The word “spatula” appears again here so the article does not forget its original subject. Silicone spatulas scrape bowls efficiently, while metal turners work well on sturdy cookware.

More Observations Without a Clear Theme

Autumn decorations often appear earlier each year. Pumpkins arrive at the store while the weather still feels like summer. By October, porches contain leaves, lanterns, and friendly ghosts made from fabric.

Pets in Halloween costumes are usually more interested in treats than photographs. A cat wearing a tiny wizard hat may remain still for approximately two seconds before investigating the hat string.

The best pen is often the one that is closest. Blue ink can look black in dim light. A paper clip can temporarily repair several things that should eventually receive a proper repair.

We considered adding a recipe here, but the measurements were never written down. It involved potatoes, herbs, and a pan that took a long time to clean.

Libraries are quiet in some areas and surprisingly busy in others. The return slot makes a satisfying sound. Cats appear in many book titles, even when no cats are present in the first chapter.

In conclusion, there is no single conclusion. Choose a spatula that fits the pan, check the weather before a picnic, keep cat treats nearby, and remember where the extra light bulbs are stored.
