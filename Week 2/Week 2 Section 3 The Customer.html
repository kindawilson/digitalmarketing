The Buyer Persona

You may have looked at this aspect while considering positioning. The two elements go hand in hand: What does your customer need, and what unique solution are you providing?

It is important not to skim through this part. Delve into the facets of the customer. What problems do they have that your product can solve? Where are they looking for information? What are their habits throughout the day?

As we look at these items, we will develop a buyer persona. A buyer persona is a semi-fictional visual representation of the ideal customer. It is as if we took what we know about the target market, put it into the form of a person, and brought it to life. This helps a marketer visualize the customer and design more effective marketing.

A buyer persona can include

  • Demographics, such as occupation and income
  • Lifestyle, including housing, transportation, and hobbies
  • Key pain points or the problem the product solves
  • Fears or objections to using the product
  • Media and social media habits
  • Online research and search terms
  • A “day in the life” or typical calendar

For digital marketing, we will particularly focus on social media habits and online research or search terms.

Base the buyer persona on real research. If you do not have all the information you need, include what you know and develop it further as you conduct primary research and obtain more analytics.

You may have used something similar called a customer avatar. Some people distinguish the two by saying the avatar is more story-based and the buyer persona is more data-driven. For this framework, we are using the buyer persona as a single tool to represent our customer.

The Customer Journey

As we focus on the customer, it is important to look at the customer journey: What journey do customers typically take to find and purchase from us? What customer journey do we want them to take?

If you have not heard the term before, it is an intuitive concept. The customer journey is the complete process a customer takes from never having heard of the product to purchasing it and then becoming a loyal customer.

Creating a Customer Journey Map

We will focus on the first three stages for this particular map. To begin, think through and plot the specific steps that a customer might take on the road to purchasing your product.

It can help to map every step that one specific customer might take. At each point, identify the touchpoint A touchpoint is any point of contact that a brand has with a customer. It can be digital, such as a website, kiosk, ad, or social media post. It can also be tangible, such as a greeting by a salesperson or a thank-you card in the mail.. After you map the journey, you can put the touchpoints in their respective stage columns.
